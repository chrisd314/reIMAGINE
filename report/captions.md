# Figure captions

Generated by the pipeline; numbers are the macros of `report/numbers.tex`.

**Figure 1. The map of world folklore.** (a) 1,462 English folktale versions linked by text similarity (TF-IDF; top 10% of pairs, 106,799 edges), placed by ForceAtlas2 on the full network and coloured by Louvain community (the three largest in colour, the others grey and labelled). Lines show the disparity backbone of the complete similarity graph (α = 0.01; 2,495 edges); communities are computed on the full network, not on the backbone. (b) The communities and their most distinctive words (TF-IDF lift). (c, d) Agreement of the communities with tale type, recorded region and source collection on the 978 tales that carry all three: NMI (c) and attribute assortativity (d), against 1,000 label shuffles (dark bars, middle 95%) and 1,000 shuffles within source collection (light bars).

**Figure 2. Where parables land.** (a) For each tradition, the share of the parables' links (10 nearest folktales each) to every community of Figure 1 minus the same share for non-story text of the same translation (percentage points), with 95% bootstrap intervals; * marks cells beyond 1,000 within-tradition label shuffles after Bonferroni correction. Below each panel: the tale types most often voted by the parables' nearest folktales. (b) Median folk-closeness (mean cosine to the 5 nearest folktales) of the parables minus that of their baseline (blue; filled: permutation p < 0.05) and minus that of length-matched folktale excerpts (orange; filled: 95% interval excludes 0). Parables are closer than their baseline for Buddhist, Taoist and Jewish; every tradition's parables are less folktale-like than folktale excerpts of the same length. TF-IDF; Supplementary Figure S2 repeats the figure with the other representation.

**Figure 3. Validity.** (a) Share of the 36 religious retellings of known tale type (held out of the map) for which a folktale of the same type is among the 10 nearest: TF-IDF 0.69, embeddings 0.61, TF-IDF with nouns masked 0.47; bars are 95% bootstrap intervals, the dashed line the analytic chance rate. (b) Share for which the type voted by the 10 nearest folktales is the right one; grey band: middle 95% of random draws. (c) Hand-label precision and Cohen's κ (pending until two coders have labelled 100 top-1 pairs).

**Supplementary Figure S1. Tale type, region and source collection across edge constructions.** NMI between the communities and each label minus the mean of 1,000 label shuffles (filled) and of 1,000 shuffles within source collection (open), for graphs built by density (5%, 10%, 15%), by k nearest neighbours (k = 5, 10, 20) and from texts with names and collection/translator formulae masked. Shaded: the canonical graph. (a) TF-IDF; (b) embeddings.

**Supplementary Figure S2.** Figure 2 with the second representation (embeddings), on the embedding map's communities.

**Table 1. Robustness of the headline.** For each check, whether the per-tradition verdicts of the headline (closer than the same-translation baseline; community profile different from the baseline's) stay the same, and the key number. Rule: yes if all comparable verdicts agree, partly if at least 60%, no otherwise. Data: `results/table1_robustness.csv`.
