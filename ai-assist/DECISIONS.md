# Decisions

Every judgement call behind the analysis and the website, one line each, with its reason. Settings live in `analysis.yaml` and `config.yaml`. Where a decision is a setting, change it there and rerun. Decisions that were later dropped are in the git history. "Spec" is the task description of 11 November 2026.

## Corpus

| # | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | The calibration set is the 36 versions found by the audit screen of 4 November, listed in `data/mappings/calibration_set.csv`. | Spec. The file is the single reviewable list. |
| C2 | The network keeps the 5 folktales with conflicting ATU labels and the 335 without a region, as nodes without that label. | They are folktales. Only the label tests need labels. |
| C3 | `n_tokens` = whitespace-delimited words, for parables and folktales alike. | The same definition as the corpus's `n_words`. |
| C4 | The spec says "1,498 versions with ATU type and place". 1,493 have one ATU type and 1,133 a mapped place and region. The network uses all 1,462 folktales. The label tests use the 978 that carry a tale type, a region and a source collection. | One node set keeps the three labels on the same scale. |
| C5 | **The calibration test is not circular.** No calibration ID and no 5-word-shingle near-duplicate of one is in the pool (checked in `load_corpus`, so in every stage), and no pool folktale has a cosine above 0.9 to a retelling in either representation (checked in the `data` and `checks` stages). Highest cosines: TF-IDF 0.380, embeddings 0.894 (`results/calibration_exclusion.json`). | The near-copy limit 0.9 is the same as the parable duplicate rule P3. |
| C6 | **Source collection = the corpus's `source_key`** (author or editor of the cited book, as parsed by the cleaning stage). It is known for 1,284 of 1,462 folktales (564 collections, 55 of them with at least 5 tales covering 576 tales, 383 with one tale). The 178 tales without a source stay unknown. Recovering them from the notes was tried and dropped, because only 10 notes hold a parsable reference. **Translator** = a "translated … by X" in the source or notes, stated for 168 tales only. Translator is therefore not tested as a label of its own. It enters through the collection (a book has one translator) and through the masking of translator formulae. | Coverage is logged so the reader knows what the collection test can see. |

## Text representations

| # | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | The TF-IDF vocabulary and IDF are fitted on the 1,462 folktales, and every other text is projected into that space. Words that no folktale uses are ignored. | Spec. Adding parables must not change the network. |
| R2 | The embedding cache stays in `data/interim/cache/` (per chunk, and per text). | Reuses the existing cache, so no folktale is encoded twice. |
| R3 | **Main representation: TF-IDF.** Rule: the representation with the higher calibration recall@10. TF-IDF 0.69, embeddings 0.61. | Fixed by the gate number alone. TF-IDF is also less tied to text length (Spearman 0.36 against 0.61) and less hub-prone. Both representations are reported everywhere. |
| R4 | Name masking uses the capitalised-word rule of course week 6 over all texts of the study, plus a list of story openings, scriptural formulae and editors' notes in `analysis.yaml`. | A simple rule that can be reviewed word by word. |
| R5 | Masked words are removed, not replaced by a visible token. | A token such as "X" is itself shared by every text with names. Masked and unmasked embedding closeness correlated 0.77 with "X" and 0.88 with removal. For TF-IDF both give the same result. |
| R6 | **Noun masking:** tokens tagged NOUN or PROPN by spaCy `en_core_web_sm` are replaced by "[N]", which the TF-IDF tokenizer ignores (one letter). Everything else stays. TF-IDF only, because embeddings of noun-less text are not sentences the model was trained on. Neighbours are placed on the unmasked TF-IDF communities. Raw closeness is not comparable between the two spaces, so the comparison is parables against baseline inside the masked space. | The check asks whether a resemblance rests on shared characters. |
| R7 | A word counts as a noun when spaCy tags it NOUN or PROPN in at least half of its uses over all texts of the study (16,400 words). Match drivers are the 5 terms with the largest product of TF-IDF weights in a text's closest pair, with the share of the whole cosine that comes from nouns. | One tag per word keeps the masking consistent across texts. |
| R8 | **Translator formulae** (`analysis.yaml`, `masking.translator_formulae`): a scan for 5-word phrases in at least 3 tales, with at least 60% of them in one collection, found the Panchatantra opening "In a certain place there lived" (5 tales), the Grimm header "Kinder- und Hausmärchen (Children's and Household Tales -- Grimms' Fairy Tales)" (4) and an editor's note on the Panchatantra (5 tales, 6 sentences). The other hits were shared plot phrases or near-copies of one tale. They are masked together with names and openings. | A book's fixed phrases could make its tales look alike. The scan also found the editors' notes of N3. |
| R9 | spaCy 3.8 and `en_core_web_sm` 3.8.0 are in a separate dependency group, `spacy` (`uv sync --group spacy`). | Only the noun masking needs them. The website deploy needs neither, and the noun-masking tests run on toy tagged tokens. |

## The folktale network

| # | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | Louvain seeds 42, 7 and 19. The partition of seed 42 at 10% density is the main one. | The frozen configuration already used these seeds, with 42 first. |
| A2 | Modularity null: an edge-swap routine with 10 swaps per edge and weights reassigned at random. It runs 100 shuffles at 10% density and 20 at 5% and 15%. | The same rule as `networkx.double_edge_swap`, which took hours here. Fewer shuffles at the other densities keep the run near 15 minutes. |
| A3 | Shuffles run in parallel processes, each with its own seeded random stream, and are cached per graph. | Results do not depend on the number of workers, and reruns take 2 minutes. |
| A4 | Communities with fewer than 1% of the tales (isolates and tiny groups) are reported together as "small groups". | TF-IDF: 12 tales. Embeddings: 168 tales, 117 of them isolates. |
| A5 | A community's words are those with the highest mean TF-IDF weight in the community relative to all tales, without name-like words. The course definition (the community as one document) is shown as well. | With 5 to 9 large communities almost every word occurs in each, so the IDF over communities is 0 for the words that describe them. |
| A6 | LDA: k = the number of communities with at least 1% of the tales, counts without stop words and name-like words, words in at least 5 tales, 5,000 terms, 30 iterations, seed 0. | "k ≈ number of communities" in the spec. |
| A7 | **Community names.** The nine large communities of the main TF-IDF network carry names chosen by the authors: C1 Royal wonder tales, C2 Animal fables, C3 Domestic & trickster, C4 Household & youth, C5 Devil & supernatural, C6 Family & moral fate, C7 Archaic-register tales, C8 Insect & nature fables, C9 Satirical & monkey fables. They are set in `analysis.yaml`, each with the first top word of its community, which the network stage checks. Embedding communities and the other seeds keep their ids. | Names read from each community's dominant tale types and words make the network readable. The check stops a changed partition from being mislabelled. |
| A8 | The disparity filter keeps 73 (α = 0.05) and 22 (α = 0.01) edges of the thresholded 10% graph, whose weights are nearly uniform per tale, and 11,272 and 2,495 edges of the complete similarity graph. The maps draw each tale's strongest links. The communities always come from the full 10% graph. | Edge weights in a thresholded similarity graph are nearly uniform per tale. Embedding cosines are too uniform for any backbone (at most 2 edges). |
| A9 | Node positions come from ForceAtlas2 on the whole 10% graph (linear attraction, 300 iterations), not from a backbone. | A backbone's layout mixed the communities, while the whole graph keeps them apart. This affects the drawing only. |
| A10 | PageRank z-scores are listed only for tales in the largest component with at least the median degree. | A tale with a few edges has almost no spread under the shuffles, so its z-score is huge and meaningless. |
| A11 | **Edge constructions:** densities 5%, 10% and 15%, and symmetrised k-nearest-neighbour graphs with k = 5, 10 and 20 (an edge if either tale is among the other's k most similar, ties to the lower index, positive cosines only), for both representations. Density graphs reuse the modularity nulls of A2, each kNN graph gets 20 shuffles. Tale type, region and source collection are tested on the 978 tales of C4 with 1,000 label shuffles. | Edges are a modelling choice, so every test is repeated on twelve graphs. |
| A12 | **Within-collection null:** type and region labels shuffled only among tales of the same source collection (1,000 times). Tales without a collection are outside these tests, and a collection with one tale never moves, which makes the null strict. | A book's style could fake both type and region. |
| A13 | **Masked network:** names, openings and translator formulae masked in every folktale, TF-IDF refitted and embeddings recomputed on the masked texts, 10% graph, Louvain seed 42, the same label tests and nulls. | Tests whether the communities rest on names and editors' phrases. |
| A14 | **Structure against nulls:** the degree distribution against 20 random graphs G(n, m) of the same size, and clustering (unweighted, and Onnela's weighted version, checked against networkx in a test) against the first 20 shuffles of the modularity null (same seed and stream). TF-IDF clustering is far above a random graph of the same density but below the degree-preserving null: the hub-heavy degree sequence alone makes many triangles. | Reported as found. |
| A15 | Community table: the words of A5, the name of A7, the most common LDA topic with its share, and the three most frequent tale types, regions and source collections with counts. | One table describes each community. |

## Parables and baseline

| # | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | Raw pages are cached compressed in `data/raw/parables/<tradition>/` and git-ignored. `fetch_log.csv` (URL, time, SHA-256) and `parables.csv` are committed. | Third-party page markup stays local. The texts are public domain. |
| P2 | Length limits: 40 to 3,000 tokens. | Shorter segments are cross-references and longer ones are epics (five Jātaka of 3,000 to 11,000 tokens were skipped). |
| P3 | A new story is dropped when it is a calibration version: the same story number (Jātaka number, or Masnavi book and story), the same title, or a TF-IDF cosine above 0.9. | Spec, plus the story number where the corpus prints it. Five Jātaka numbers were identified from titles (marked in `calibration_set.csv`). Dropped: Jātaka 322 and 44, Masnavi II.13, IV.5 and VI.7. |
| P4 | Where more stories qualify than the target, a seeded random draw of the target size is kept (Sufi 60 of 78, Taoist 50 of 115, Jātaka 72 of 547). | Similar sizes per tradition. Set a target to `all` in `analysis.yaml` to keep everything. |
| P5 | **Christian.** World English Bible (eBible.org, `engwebp`). 40 parables with verse ranges in `data/mappings/parables/christian_refs.yaml`. The longest of parallel tellings is kept. | Spec. |
| P6 | Christian ranges hold the story only (explanations such as Matthew 13:18–23 are left out). Mustard Seed with Leaven, and Hidden Treasure with Pearl, are one text each. John 10 and 15 are not included. | Each half of the two pairs is under 40 words, and John's are discourses. |
| P7 | **Buddhist.** 72 Jātaka drawn at random from all 547 (Cowell edition), plus the 9 prose parables of the Lotus Sutra (Kern), cut where the speaker turns to the application. | Spec. |
| P8 | Jātaka: the story of the past runs from the first paragraph that opens "Once on a time" (or a like formula) to the printed rule or the closing formula ("identified the Birth", "his lesson ended"). 1 of 72 could not be cut and is kept whole with a note. | Spec. |
| P9 | **Jewish.** Rapaport's book is a list of passages, each closed by a source reference, and one passage is one item. Items that pass the story rule and have at least 60 tokens are the parables (50, all that qualify). | The target of 60 was not reached, and lowering the length limit let sayings in. |
| P10 | The two retold legend cycles at the front of Rapaport's book (Alexander, Ashmedai) are not used. | They have no source references to cut them by. |
| P11 | **Sufi.** Per Masnavi story, the parable is Whinfield's prose summary and his translated couplets are the baseline. | The summary is the tale, and the couplets are mostly the teaching hung on it. Some couplets are narrative, which makes the baseline a little too story-like (conservative). |
| P12 | **Taoist.** One numbered section of Legge's Zhuangzi is one item. Books XXX and XXXI are undivided and are one item each. | Legge's own division. |
| P13 | Story rule for the Midrash and the Zhuangzi (`reimagine/parables/narrative.py`): enough past-tense action verbs, plus reported speech or a story opener, and for the Midrash also little exegesis. It never looks at folktales. | Both sources mix stories with sayings. A rule that used similarity to folktales would bias the result. |
| P14 | The rule is strict. Of the proposal's three Zhuangzi examples, the butterfly dream and the useless tree pass it and Cook Ding does not. The random draw of 50 includes a useless-tree story (Book IV) but not the butterfly dream. | A looser rule let discursive sections in. |
| P15 | Baseline passages are consecutive units of non-story text (verses, sayings, couplet lines), with lengths set to evenly spaced quantiles of the tradition's parable lengths and spread over the whole source. | Length matters for similarity, so length is matched first. |
| P16 | Where the source is too short, fewer passages are cut rather than shorter ones: Buddhist 24 (Dhammapada), Taoist 24 (Tao Te Ching), the others 40. | See P15. |
| P17 | Jewish baseline = Rapaport's short items without action or speech, and Pirqe Aboth (Taylor). Christian = Proverbs and the Epistles. The Buddhist baseline is Müller's Dhammapada, a different translation from the parables, because the Jātaka has no non-story text. | Spec. The Buddhist pairing is the weakest and is stated as a limitation. |
| P18 | No Hindu tradition. | Optional in the proposal. |

## Placement and the main test

| # | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | Folktale scale: each folktale's folk-closeness to all other folktales. A second value leaves out tales of its own type. | A folktale's nearest tales are often versions of the same tale, but a parable has none. |
| B2 | Intervals: 10,000 bootstrap resamples, percentile method. | Spec. |
| B3 | The share of parables above the 95th percentile of their own baseline is reported next to the test. | The proposal's "above this band" needed a definition. |
| B4 | Length adjustment: a text's folk-closeness minus the median folk-closeness of folktales of the same length (15 quantile bins of log length, interpolated). | Closeness rises with length in both representations. |
| B5 | **Main test.** Per tradition, the parable/baseline label is shuffled among that tradition's texts (1,000 times, `common.permute_within`), with the text as the unit. "Closer than baseline" uses the one-sided p (parables closer), because the question is directional. The two-sided p is in the table too. The 95% interval resamples parables and baseline passages separately. | With the text as the unit, the 10 links of one parable are not treated as independent draws. |
| B6 | The permutation p and the bootstrap interval can disagree near the edge (Jewish, TF-IDF: p = 0.020, interval −0.002 to +0.018). The verdict follows the permutation test and both are reported. | The permutation test is the test, the interval describes the size. |
| B7 | Community profile: each text's 10 nearest folktales, counted per community of the same representation (TF-IDF communities for TF-IDF and its noun-masked variant, embedding communities for embeddings). Small groups are one column. Cells are marked after Bonferroni correction over all tradition × community cells of a variant (50 for TF-IDF, 30 for embeddings). | The same partition as the network. |
| B8 | **Type vote:** the ATU type with the largest summed cosine among a text's 10 nearest folktales. Ties go to the type of the nearer folktale, and folktales without one type do not vote. | Summing cosines lets two moderately close versions of one type outvote a single closer tale. |
| B9 | Top source collection of a tradition = the collection with the largest share of the tradition's links, among links to folktales with a known collection, compared with that collection's share of the network and of the baseline's links. | Tests whether one book dominates a tradition's neighbours. |
| B10 | **Length-matched excerpts.** For a text of n words, every folktale with at least 0.9 n words is eligible. 5 distinct eligible tales are drawn at random, and from each an excerpt of min(n, tale length) words is cut, starting at a random sentence start that leaves room. Excerpts are drawn for parables and baseline passages (2,245 in all). An excerpt's folk-closeness leaves out its own source tale and that tale's near-copies. The comparison is paired: a text's closeness minus the mean of its own 5 excerpts. | Sentence starts keep excerpts comparable to parables, which open at a sentence. |
| B11 | **Length regression:** closeness = slope × (log words − median parable log words) + one level per group, no intercept. The groups are each tradition's parables, each tradition's baseline and the folktale excerpts. Robust (HC1) standard errors. A second model on parables only (log words + tradition) asks whether the order of the traditions holds at equal length (Kendall τ with the raw order). | Levels at a common length are directly "closeness at equal length". |
| B12 | Baseline passages count as length-matched when a Mann-Whitney test of parable against baseline lengths gives p ≥ 0.05. All five traditions pass (p = 0.29 to 0.94, baseline medians 0.99 to 1.11 times the parable medians). | A check that the matching of P15 worked. |

## Checks

| # | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| K1 | Gate rule: recall@10 is "clearly above chance" when its bootstrap 95% interval lies above the analytic chance rate and the chance of that many hits (Poisson-binomial) is below 0.001. | The spec's wording needed a number. Both representations pass. |
| K2 | Hubs = the 10 folktales that occur most often in the parables' top-10 lists, per representation. | Spec. |
| K3 | **Type-vote accuracy** of the 36 retellings: the share whose voted type is their own. Chance: each query keeps its 10 cosines but its neighbours are 10 random folktales, 1,000 draws (mean and middle 95%). | The vote needed a chance level. |
| K4 | **Rule for the table of checks**, fixed in `reimagine/robustness.py` before the table was first computed. Per tradition the main result is two verdicts: closer than baseline (one-sided permutation p < 0.05) and a profile different from the baseline's (JSD permutation p < 0.05). A check holds "yes" if every comparable verdict agrees with the main result, "partly" if at least 60% agree, and otherwise "no". Edge construction compares only the profile verdict (closeness needs no graph). Length compares only the closeness verdict. Source collection is "yes" if tale type beats the within-collection null and no tradition sends more than 25% of its links to one collection. | "Does the main result hold (yes, partly or no)" needed a rule. |
| K5 | **No hand validation.** No parable–folktale pair is checked by hand. Validity rests on the 36 held-out retellings of known type (a labelled reference set taken from the ATU index), the label shuffles and the second representation. | Decision of the authors (18 November). The course names three ways to validate a measurement: against a null, against another method, and against a hand-labelled sample. The first two are used, and the held-out retellings stand in for the third. |

## Website

| # | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| W1 | **Three data files:** `story.json` (results of both representations and every text), `network.json` (both networks) and `near.json` (loaded only when a reading panel needs it), written by the `site` stage. `web/scripts/copy-data.mjs` copies `data/site/` into the build, so the deploy workflow builds the site without rerunning the analysis and refuses to publish an opening over 40 words. | Each page loads only what it needs, and no number is typed by hand. |
| W2 | **Pages:** Overview (the questions, the three steps, the main result, every check at once, the answer), Folktales (tale types, similarity, the network against random graphs, Louvain communities, story, place or book, edges as a modelling choice, central tales), Parables (texts and baseline, the held-out test, closer than the baseline, where they land, what the resemblance rests on, the checks), Explore and Methods. Every section states what was done, why, and the course week, then the result. | The project outline has three parts: folktales and tale types, communities, parables. |
| W3 | **The main figure** on the Overview: for each tradition, the share of its parables' links to each folktale community minus its baseline's, with significant bars solid, the closeness verdict and the most-voted tale type. The network map follows the course's drawing rules: a backbone, colour by community, size by degree, labels on the most central tales. | One figure carries the main result. |
| W4 | **Exploration everywhere:** a zoomable network of both representations with search, colouring by community, type, region or book and sizing by links or PageRank, parables placed at the mean position of their five nearest folktales, a switch between TF-IDF and embeddings in every section, and a reading panel for any tale or text with its ten nearest folktales and the words behind each match. | The analysis should be explorable, not only described. |
| W5 | **Shuffles in the browser.** The label test and the permutation test can be rerun by the reader on the same labels, tales and statistic as the pipeline, so the observed values equal the published ones (`tests/test_site.py` asserts this for the label test). The browser's own p-values are approximate and shown next to the pipeline's. | Course weeks 2 and 3: the permutation logic, made visible. |
| W6 | **Links to the full texts.** `reimagine/folktexts.py` applies the corpus scraper's own rules to the collection's two index pages, so 1,423 of 1,457 typed folktales and 35 of 36 retellings link to the exact Folktexts page, opened at the tale's title. Every parable and passage links to its page at sacred-texts.com or eBible.org. | Readers can read any pair of texts in full at the source. |
| W7 | **The research questions, the community names and the baseline** are worded the same way everywhere: the questions do not presuppose communities, the nine large TF-IDF communities carry the names of A7, and the baseline is described as non-story text from the same tradition, from the same translation wherever possible. | One wording for the site, the repository and the project's write-up. |
| W8 | Charts use three categorical colours and one neutral, with a dark mode, and every chart has its numbers in a table underneath. | Legibility. |
